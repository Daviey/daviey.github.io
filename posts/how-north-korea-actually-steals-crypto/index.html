How North Korea Actually Steals Crypto

Engineer: Weekly sweep. Moving 401,000 ETH to cold storage, same as every fortnight.

Security: Interface shows the transfer. Simulation matches.

Engineer: Hardware wallet wants a signature over a blob of hex. It always does.

Security: Sign it then. Lunch?

CISO: Approved. Quiet week.

Three signers at Bybit approved roughly that transaction on 21 February 2025, and about $1.5bn left the exchange within minutes. The screen told them the truth. The keys signed a lie. Nearly every large North Korean crypto theft since 2019 contains some version of that gap, and the gap is where the money goes.

This is a sourced tour of how the DPRK actually steals digital assets. The running totals, the people-attacks that open the door, the kill chains inside six named heists, the laundering pipeline behind them, and the defences that held up when tested. Every claim carries a source and a date, because a fair chunk of what everyone knows about this subject is wrong, and I will flag the wrong bits as we pass them.

"Nobody broke the cryptography. They broke the screen between the signers and the keys."

The Score So Far

The UN Panel of Experts, in the last report before Russia vetoed its mandate out of existence in March 2024, counted 58 attacks on crypto platforms between 2017 and 2023 worth roughly $3bn, and assessed that illicit cyber activity funds about 40 percent of the DPRK's weapons of mass destruction programmes and half its foreign currency income (S/2024/215, March 2024). Its successor, the 11-nation Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team, put theft at $2.8bn for January 2024 through September 2025 alone (MSMT report, October 2025). Chainalysis carries an all-time lower bound of $6.75bn (December 2025), and TRM Labs crossed $6bn cumulative in April 2026 (TRM Labs, 30 April 2026).

The shape of the curve matters more than the totals. DPRK share of all hacked value has climbed from 22 percent in 2022 to 37, 39, then 64 percent in 2025, and 76 percent for early 2026, achieved with two attacks (TRM, same report). In 2025 the group stole a record $2.02bn while running 74 percent fewer attacks than the year before (Chainalysis, December 2025, above). Fewer, bigger, better-rehearsed. That is an industrial operation maturing, not a crime wave.

The People Pipeline

Every kill chain below starts with a person, and the people-attacks run on an industrialised fake-hiring pipeline that vendors track as Contagious Interview (Unit 42's name, in use since late 2023), DeceptiveDevelopment (ESET), Famous Chollima (CrowdStrike), or Nickel Alley (Sophos) (Unit 42, November 2024, Silent Push, April 2025).

The shape is consistent. A recruiter contacts a developer or exchange employee on LinkedIn with a plausible package. Weeks of messaging, sometimes a real interview process, then a pivot to Telegram or WhatsApp where the company's IT rules stop applying. The lure arrives as a coding test. The repository depends on trojanised npm packages whose post-install scripts drop the first-stage malware. If the script fails, the page helpfully suggests a terminal command to fix it, the ClickFix pattern. Current variants hide payloads inside SVG images in Next.js challenge repos (Elastic Security Labs, July 2026).

The front companies are built to survive a phone check. Silent Push documented three, including BlockNovas LLC, which claimed a 14-person team and 12 years of operating history despite a 2024 domain registration, complete with AI-generated profile photos, an Astrill VPN and residential proxies for operator opsec, and a status dashboard running Hashtopolis password cracking on one of its own mail subdomains. The FBI seized blocknovas.com on 23 April 2025 (Silent Push, above, The Hacker News).

The malware chain behind it has three recurring stages. BeaverTail, a JavaScript loader and infostealer that grabs browser credentials and about ten crypto wallet extensions, then loads stage two. InvisibleFerret, a Python backdoor with keylogging, file exfiltration, and an AnyDesk install for hands-on access (Unit 42 and GitLab, September 2025). OtterCookie, which arrived around September 2024 and specialises in session cookies, which is MFA bypass without touching anyone's 2FA (NTT Security Holdings, Talos).

The delivery vehicle keeps climbing the supply chain. The 2022 TraderTraitor advisory already documented trojanised Electron apps like CryptAIS and AlticGO whose update check fetched the Manuscrypt RAT (CISA AA22-108A). In 2023 the same actors poisoned 3CX's desktop app, a mass-reach compromise inside which the Gopuram backdoor was deployed at exactly two cryptocurrency companies (Kaspersky, April 2023). By late 2025 they were social-engineering maintainers of mainstream npm packages directly, with takeovers of debug and chalk in September 2025 and axios in March 2026, attributed by AWS threat intelligence to the same cluster with medium confidence (AWS, July 2026). Your npm install is in the blast radius now.

Two adjacent vectors complete the picture. Fake meeting apps, typosquat "Zoom" sites where the victim is told to install a "one-time password client", delivering the Realst stealer against Web3 devs and KOLs (Cado Security on the Meeten family). And in February 2026 Mandiant documented the sharpest version, a compromised Telegram account, a Calendly link to a spoofed Zoom domain, a video of another company's CEO that may have been AI-generated, then an "audio troubleshooting" prompt that ran a ClickFix chain and installed seven macOS malware families harvesting keychains, browsers, Telegram sessions and SSH keys (Mandiant on UNC1069, February 2026). Kaspersky's GhostCall and GhostHire writeups catch BlueNoroff editing lure imagery with GPT-4o (Kaspersky, November 2025). Microsoft's April 2026 Sapphire Sleet writeup walks a fake recruiter leading to a "Zoom SDK Update" AppleScript with the malicious logic a few thousand blank lines below the decoy, a fake password dialog that validates against the local auth database, and exfiltration of Ledger Live and Exodus directories via Telegram's own bot API (Microsoft, April 2026).

The insider variant replaces the malware with a paycheck. DOJ indicted 14 North Korean nationals in December 2024 over a six-year remote-IT-worker scheme through China and Russia-based fronts that grossed at least $88m, with monthly earning quotas of $10k and source-code theft followed by extortion (DOJ, December 2024). In July 2025 a coordinated action searched 29 laptop farms across 16 states (DOJ, July 2025). Chainalysis attributes part of 2025's record haul to IT workers embedded inside exchanges, custodians and web3 firms (December 2025, above). And TRM's Drift writeup describes months of social engineering that included in-person meetings with Drift staff by proxies posing as a quant trading firm. The pipeline now shakes hands.

The volume end of the same pipeline runs against individual developers. Expel's HexagonalRodent case, January to March 2026, counted some 2,700 compromised machines and 31 operators split across six specialist teams, working AI-generated recruiter personas and coding assessments whose VS Code tasks.json fired malware the moment the folder opened. The take was described as keys to wallets holding up to $12m, an exposure figure rather than confirmed theft (Help Net Security, April 2026).

Six Kill Chains, One Gap

Bybit, February 2025. A Safe{Wallet} developer's macOS machine was compromised on 4 February via a trojanised project, yielding AWS session tokens. On 19 February attacker JavaScript was injected into the S3-hosted Safe frontend, written to stay dormant unless a Bybit cold wallet address was loaded. On the 21st, three signers saw an honest-looking transfer while their hardware wallets signed a delegatecall that swapped the multisig's implementation contract. The JavaScript removed itself after the drain (Sygnia case study, Safe Ecosystem Foundation, March 2025). No Safe smart contract bug, no Bybit infrastructure breach. Trail of Bits' postmortem maths is the whole article in one line, the failure modes were delegatecall plus blind signing, and the fix is verification on a machine that isn't the signing machine (Trail of Bits, February 2025).

Radiant, October 2024. INLETDRIFT sat on the devices of developers holding 3-of-11 multisig authority. During a routine emissions adjustment the malware altered what the Safe UI displayed. The signers ran Tenderly simulations and manually reviewed transactions. Both checks validated the transaction on the screen, not the one in the calldata (Radiant postmortem, December 2024). Radiant never recovered and wound the protocol down in June 2026 (The Block).

Ronin, March 2022. A fake recruiter's job-offer PDF compromised a Sky Mavis engineer and took four of nine validator keys. The bridge needed five. The fifth came from a November 2021 favour, during a gas spike Axie DAO had allowlisted Sky Mavis to sign on its behalf, and nobody revoked it. Four stolen keys plus one stale trust artifact cleared the threshold, and the withdrawal sat unnoticed for six days (SlowMist exploit report, March 2022).

DMM Bitcoin, May 2024. The FBI's cleanest documented chain. A LinkedIn recruiter approach to an employee of wallet vendor Ginco, a malicious Python "pre-employment test" on GitHub, stolen session cookies, then manipulation of a legitimate transaction inside DMM's own operations. $308m gone without a key being cryptographically extracted (FBI, December 2024).

Drift, April 2026. Social engineering, including those in-person meetings, got Security Council signers to pre-authorise transactions via Solana durable nonces, which stretch validity from roughly 90 seconds to indefinite. On 27 March Drift migrated its council to a 2-of-5 threshold with zero timelock. The attacker minted a wash-traded fake token that the oracles accepted as collateral, then executed 31 withdrawals in about 12 minutes (TRM, April 2026).

KelpDAO, April 2026. Two internal RPC nodes were compromised and fed false chain data, a DDoS on the external RPCs forced failover onto the poisoned pair, and a bridge with a single configured verifier accepted a fabricated burn message. No signatures stolen at all (TRM, 30 April 2026).

Read as a set, the taxonomy is about trust artifacts rather than keys. The signing UI and its supply chain (Bybit, WazirX). The display/sign gap under blind signing (Bybit, Radiant). The signer endpoint (Radiant, Ronin, DMM, Sapphire Sleet's targets). Threshold and allowlist hygiene (Ronin, Harmony, Drift). Verifier quorums and the data they trust (KelpDAO). The private key itself, the thing everyone bought HSMs for, is the component they touch least.

Following the Money

Chainalysis clocks the laundering cycle at about 45 days. Days zero to five, DeFi and mixers. Days six to ten, no-KYC exchanges. Days 20 to 45, final integration through guarantee services and Chinese-language OTC brokers, in tranches under $500k (Chainalysis, December 2025). DPRK uniquely over-uses bridges, mixers and Chinese-language services relative to every other thief on chain (same report).

Bybit was the best-observed run. Over $160m moved within 48 hours, all of it off the original addresses within ten days, the vast majority converted ETH to BTC through THORChain, whose validators failed to agree on a halt, a three-vote Mimir pause overturned within minutes. Bybit's CEO put THORChain's share at roughly 72 percent (TRM, March 2025, BeInCrypto).

The mixer layer is a sanctions arms race in miniature. Blender.io, sanctioned May 2022. Tornado Cash, sanctioned August 2022. Lazarus moved to Sinbad, which the FBI and Dutch police seized in November 2023, on to YoMix, and then back to Tornado Cash in March 2024 because immutable contracts cannot be seized (Elliptic, March 2024). A US appeals court then held in November 2024 that immutable contracts aren't sanctionable property, and OFAC delisted Tornado Cash in March 2025 (Treasury). The developers fared differently. Alexey Pertsev got 64 months in a Dutch court in May 2024, and Roman Storm was convicted in August 2025 on the unlicensed money-transmitting count with a 2027 retrial on the rest (DOJ SDNY).

The cash-out end runs through named people and places. DOJ indicted Sim Hyon Sop, a Foreign Trade Bank representative, with OTC traders Wu Huihui and Cheng Hung Man in April 2023 for converting heist proceeds through Hong Kong front companies, with purchases that included missile components (DOJ). KelpDAO's pre-attack funding traces back to a wallet tied to Wu Huihui (TRM, April 2026, above). Cambodia's Huione Guarantee, a Telegram marketplace, processed an estimated $27bn before FinCEN designated it a primary money laundering concern in May 2025 citing at least $37.6m in received DPRK-heist assets; Telegram banned its channels, a US final rule cut it off in October 2025, and DOJ seized its backend infrastructure in June 2026 (Elliptic, Federal Register, DOJ). The volume moved to successor markets like Tudou Guarantee within days. The MSMT has documented USDT priced directly into attempted weapons and gold deals, including a MANPADS contract above $10m (MSMT via 38 North, March 2026).

Recovery is the number that should humble everyone. Attribution of Bybit took under five hours, ZachXBT had the Lazarus link by 19:09 UTC on day one, and $43m was frozen within two days. By August 2026 Bybit's own accounting, after a civil suit against the DPRK, the RGB and Lazarus secured a preliminary injunction across 28 venues, stood at $48.4m recovered and $30.5m frozen. About 5 percent (PRNewswire, August 2026). Detection is fast. Recovery barely happens.

Where Enforcement Actually Is

The monitoring gap is real but smaller than reported. The Panel of Experts died in March 2024 to a Russian veto, and the MSMT that replaced it has no Security Council mandate, but its October 2025 report carried the numbers quoted above and documented IT workers earning $3,500 to $10k a month under up to a dozen identities (MSMT, Chainalysis readout). US actions run from the 2018 Park Jin Hyok indictment through the 14-worker case, the 2025 laptop-farm waves, and a March 2026 OFAC package naming six individuals and two entities behind IT-worker schemes the Treasury priced at nearly $800m for 2024 alone (Treasury, March 2026). The UK opened a DPRK-focused crypto sanctions fusion cell in February 2026. Whether any of it deters an operation funding half the country's foreign currency income is a different question. Nothing so far suggests it does.

What Actually Defends

The postmortems converge on a short list, and it is almost entirely key-management process rather than new cryptography.

Clear signing over blind signing. ERC-7730 lets hardware wallets display decoded transaction meaning instead of a hex blob. Every display/sign mismatch heist, Bybit, Radiant, WazirX, becomes materially harder when the signing device can argue with the screen (Ledger's Bybit analysis, NCC Group).

Verification on an independent device. Radiant's signers did simulate and did review. The compromise lived on the signing endpoint, so the checks saw the honest transaction. The lesson is procedural and boring, the machine that verifies state changes must not be the machine that signs, and ideally not the machine that initiated the transaction either. Radiant's own post-hack changes landed exactly there, a 72-hour timelock on contract upgrades and ownership transfers plus verification on independent devices (Radiant postmortem, Paradigm).

Timelocks and threshold hygiene on configuration changes. Drift's final week was a 2-of-5 threshold with zero timelock plus pre-authorised durable nonces. A multi-day timelock on any signer, threshold or upgrade change turns that twelve-minute drain into a recoverable incident (TRM and Paradigm, above). Ronin's version is the audit of stale trust artifacts. That allowlist your predecessor created for a gas spike in 2021 is still a signature.

Treat the signing UI as critical infrastructure. Bybit's loss came through a vendor's S3 bucket. Subresource integrity, pinned frontend expectations, and real-time alerting on any change to the signing interface are unglamorous and load-bearing. So is asking what your multisig vendor's developer workstations look like.

Vet the humans, including the ones you hire. The FBI's IT-worker indicators are concrete. Astrill VPN and IPRoyal proxies, hardware remote-access devices like PiKVM, laptop farms, mouse jigglers, camera-shy video calls, flawless resumes over stolen identities, and bulk copying of company repositories to personal clouds (FBI, May 2025). Run coding challenges in disposable VMs, restrict npm post-install scripts on developer machines, and treat any interview that pivots to Telegram and a GitHub repo as an incident, because the people running them have institutional patience, AI-generated faces, and a weapons programme to feed.

The industry bodies are young but real. Crypto ISAC launched in May 2024 with Coinbase, Circle, Kraken and others. The Blockchain Security Standards Council published its first four standards in May 2025, including a key-management standard, and Mastercard joined in April 2026 (Crypto ISAC, BSSC). Bybit's own post-hack work is worth reading with clear eyes. Its three-tier withdrawal defence flagged $300m of suspicious withdrawals in Q4 2025, which is genuinely good, and it is a user-scam defence. The treasury signing path that failed has no publicly documented redesign, so treat "Bybit hardened everything" as unverified.

What I Would Tell a Team

If you hold keys that matter, assume the approach has already happened, check whether it landed on a signer, and design so that one honest human staring at a truthful screen still cannot lose the treasury alone. The DPRK's decade of thefts is a story about trust artifacts, screens, sessions, allowlists, thresholds, verifiers, and every single one of them was cheaper to attack than the keys themselves. Harden the artifacts. The keys were never the problem.
